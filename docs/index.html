The story

Why I built this

Every month, Taiwan's government gives its economy a "traffic light." Blue means the economy is weak. Red means it's running hot. Green is in the middle. It's a simple way to see how things are going without reading a pile of reports.

I studied economics in Taiwan, where this light shows up in the news every month. When I moved to Seattle for grad school, I wanted the same simple way to check the U.S. economy. But the U.S. doesn't have one.

So I made my own. Taiwan's light is built from nine numbers, like exports, stock prices and factory output. I found the nine closest U.S. numbers, scored them the same way Taiwan does, and added them up. Then I tested it: in every U.S. recession since 1993, my light turned blue.

I also had 20 years of trade data on Taiwan, the U.S. and China from an earlier project. Putting it all together tells a bigger story: Taiwan's economy is slowly moving its center from China to the U.S.

Chapter 01

How hot is each economy? Taiwan vs. the U.S.

Taiwan and the U.S., side by side

Higher score = hotter economy. Taiwan's line is the official score; the U.S. line is my version. Both dropped sharply in 2001 and 2008–09.

My U.S. light, every month since 1993

Higher score = hotter economy. The colored strip shows the light for each month. Gray bands are official U.S. recessions, and my light turned blue in all three. It also turned blue for 3 months in 2023 without a recession: U.S. factories slowed down that year, but the overall economy kept growing.

The 9 numbers behind my latest U.S. light

Each number gets 1 to 5 points: 5 if it's growing much faster than usual, 1 if it's much weaker than usual. The 9 points add up to the score out of 45. The factory survey is a score where anything above 0 means more factories say business is getting better.

What this means

Taiwan's economic indicator is showing a red light, which means the economy is doing really well. But when I looked more closely at the data in Chapter 06, I found that most of the growth comes from the electronics industry. It's like a basketball team relying on one star player. The team might be winning now, but what happens if that player gets injured?

Chapter 02

Trade: Taiwan, the U.S. and mainland China

Taiwan's exports, imports and trade surplus with its two biggest partners, 2004 to 2024. All values in billions of U.S. dollars.

Finding 1

The U.S. passed China (not counting Hong Kong) as Taiwan's top export market in 2024.

Taiwan sold $111B to the U.S. and $97B to China. Exports to the U.S. jumped 46% in 2024. The U.S. first came out ahead in December 2023, the first time since at least 2004.

Thin lines are monthly exports. Thick lines are the long-term trend, with seasonal ups and downs removed.

Finding 2

China's share of Taiwan's exports fell from a peak of 29.7% in 2020 to 20.4% in 2024.

In the same years, the U.S. share rose from 14.6% to 23.4%. Dollar sales to China peaked in 2021 at $126B.

Share of Taiwan's total exports, by year.

Finding 3

Taiwan's trade surplus with the U.S. grew tenfold, from $6B in 2004 to $65B in 2024.

Its surplus with China shrank from $42B in 2021 to $16B in 2024. Imports from mainland China grew 4.2 times over 20 years, faster than exports to China (2.6 times).

Trade surplus = exports minus imports, by year.

What this means

For most of the last 20 years, China was Taiwan's biggest customer. Now it's the U.S. That means what happens in America, like how much companies spend on tech or new trade rules, matters more to Taiwan than ever before.

Chapter 03

Products: what Taiwan sells

Taiwan's exports by product group, 2004 to 2024. "Machinery and electrical equipment" includes computers, servers and chips. All values in billions of U.S. dollars.

Finding 1

The U.S. passed China as the top buyer of Taiwan's machinery and electrical equipment in 2024.

Taiwan sold $88B of these products to the U.S. and $73B to China. Sales to the U.S. rose 68% in one year, from $52B in 2023. The last time the U.S. was ahead was 2004.

Exports of machinery and electrical equipment, by year.

Finding 2

Machinery and electrical equipment made up 79% of Taiwan's exports to the U.S. in 2024, up from 49.5% in 2018.

This one product group explains the whole jump in exports to the U.S. in 2024. Sales of everything else fell slightly, from $23.8B to $23.4B. Exports to China show the same pattern: 75% machinery in 2024, up from 41% in 2004.

Machinery and electrical equipment as a share of exports to each market, by year.

Finding 3

Taiwan's sales of optical and precision instruments to China fell 81%, from a peak of $17.7B in 2010 to $3.3B in 2024.

This product group was 23% of what Taiwan sold to China in 2010. In 2024 it was about 3%.

Exports of optical and precision instruments to China, by year.

Finding 4

Beyond machinery, Taiwan sells different things to each market: more metals and transportation equipment to the U.S., more chemicals and plastics to China.

After machinery and electrical equipment, Taiwan's biggest exports to the U.S. in 2024 were base metals ($7.1B), transportation equipment ($3.7B) and plastics and rubber ($3.2B). To China, they were chemicals ($5.8B), plastics and rubber ($5.1B) and base metals ($4.5B).

Exports by product group in 2024. Machinery and electrical equipment ($88.0B to the U.S., $72.8B to mainland China) is left out so the smaller groups can be seen.

Finding 5

Taiwan buys mostly machinery from China, but a wider mix from the U.S., including $8.7B of minerals and fuels.

Machinery and electrical equipment made up 64% of what Taiwan bought from China in 2024 ($52.0B of $80.6B), up from 43% in 2004. From the U.S., machinery was $13.5B (29%) and minerals and fuels, a group that includes oil, gas and coal, were $8.7B (19%), up from $0.2B in 2004.

Imports by product group in 2024. Some imports, $11.5B from the U.S. and $10.5B from China, are in groups outside these 10.

What this means

Almost 4 out of every 5 dollars Taiwan makes from selling to the U.S. come from one type of product: computers, chips and servers. When the world is buying tech, that's great. When it stops, Taiwan feels it fast. In 2023, orders dropped 16% in one year.

Chapter 04

Export orders: who is buying

Export orders are orders that foreign buyers place with Taiwan companies, 2003 to 2024. Some of these products are made in factories outside Taiwan, so orders are bigger than exports. In this chapter "China" includes Hong Kong. All values in billions of U.S. dollars.

Finding 1

U.S. buyers placed $194B of orders with Taiwan companies in 2024, a third of the total.

The U.S. has been the top source of orders every year since 2014. Mainland China and Hong Kong together placed $127B (21.5%). From 2007 to 2013, they were ahead of the U.S.

Export orders by where the buyer is, by year.

Finding 2

Orders from Southeast Asia grew 21 times, from $4.7B in 2003 to $98B in 2024, and passed Europe for the first time in 2024.

In 2023, when total orders fell 16%, orders from Southeast Asia rose 15%. Southeast Asia's share of all orders went from 2.8% to 16.7%.

Export orders from Southeast Asia (ASEAN) and Europe, by year.

What this means

An "export order" is when a foreign company asks a Taiwan company to make something. Orders from Southeast Asia (places like Vietnam and Thailand) grew 21 times in 20 years. My guess is that more products are now being built there instead of in China, and Taiwan companies are following.

Chapter 05

Investment: where Taiwan companies build

Overseas investment approved by Taiwan's government, 2003 to 2024. Approved amounts can differ from money actually spent. Taiwan counts investment in mainland China separately from the rest of the world. All values in billions of U.S. dollars.

Finding 1

In 2010, 84% of the overseas investment Taiwan approved went to mainland China. In 2024, it was 7.5%.

Approved investment in all other countries grew from $2.8B in 2010 to $44.9B in 2024. It nearly doubled in 2024 alone, from $23.6B.

Approved overseas investment, by year.

Finding 2

The U.S. was the top destination for Taiwan's overseas investment in 2023 and 2024, with $14.1B approved in 2024.

That is up from $0.5B in 2021. In 2024, the U.S. received nearly four times as much approved investment as mainland China ($3.7B).

Approved investment in the U.S. and in mainland China, 2016 to 2024.

Finding 3

Approved investment in mainland China fell 75%, from a peak of $14.6B in 2010 to $3.7B in 2024.

The number of approved projects fell from 3,875 in 2003 to 310 in 2024.

Number of approved investment projects in mainland China, by year.

Finding 4

In 2024, Taiwan approved about six times more investment going out ($48.6B) than coming in ($8.2B).

Of the investment coming into Taiwan, $7.9B came from foreign investors and $0.3B from mainland China.

Approved investment going out of Taiwan and coming in, by year. Investment from mainland China into Taiwan is counted from 2015, when the data starts.

Finding 5

North America's share of Taiwan's approved investment outside mainland China rose from 4% in 2021 to 40% in 2024.

North America received $17.8B in 2024, and Asia outside mainland China $14.9B (33%). Most of Latin America's $8.9B went to tax havens such as the British Virgin Islands, so it does not show where factories are built.

Approved investment outside mainland China by region, by year. Oceania and Africa are small and left out.

What this means

This is the change that surprised me most. In 2010, more than 8 out of 10 dollars Taiwan companies invested abroad went to China. In 2024, it was less than 1 in 10. The money went to the U.S. instead. Companies are building factories closer to their customers.

Chapter 06

Industry: what Taiwan's factories make

How much each manufacturing industry produces, 2004 to 2024. This is a production index (2021 = 100): it measures the amount made, not its dollar value.

Finding 1

In 2024, Taiwan made 48% more computers, electronic and optical products than in 2021, while total factory output was still 7.5% lower.

Computers, electronic and optical products reached an index of 147.8 in 2024. All manufacturing together was at 92.5. Electronic components, which include chips, were at 95.3.

Production index (2021 = 100), yearly average.

Finding 2

Electronic components grew the most of any industry: production in 2024 was 4.7 times the 2004 level.

All manufacturing together grew 1.7 times. Clothing, textiles and leather made less in 2024 than in 2004.

Change in production from 2004 to 2024, by industry. Move your mouse over a bar to see the numbers.

Finding 3

Taiwan's clothing industry made 71% less in 2024 than in 2004, and textiles 47% less.

Leather products fell the most, by 81%. These industries have been shrinking for 20 years, not only in recent years.

Production index (2021 = 100), yearly average.

What this means

Taiwan has a two-speed economy. Electronics is booming. Clothing, textiles and leather have been shrinking for 20 years. So "Taiwan's economy is red hot" is true on average, but if you work outside tech, you might not feel it at all.

Method

How it works

The U.S. light

Taiwan's National Development Council scores 9 indicators every month and adds them up. This site uses 9 matching U.S. indicators from FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis).

  1. Each indicator is the closest U.S. match to one of Taiwan's 9. For example, Taiwan uses overtime hours in industry and services, and the U.S. only publishes overtime hours for factories, so this site uses those.
  2. Each indicator is measured as its change from the same month a year earlier. The factory survey is used as is.
  3. Each indicator gets 1 to 5 points. Its bottom 10% of months from 1993 to 2019 score 1 point (blue) and its top 10% score 5 points (red).
  4. The 9 points are added up (9 to 45) and turned into a light using Taiwan's official bands.

The color bands are Taiwan's. The cutoffs for each indicator are set by this project, because Taiwan does not publish its exact formula. So this is adapted from Taiwan's method, not an official U.S. light. Unlike private recession dashboards, it uses a public government method and free public data.

The data

  • Taiwan light: the official monthly light from Taiwan's National Development Council (open data), updated automatically every week.
  • U.S. light: FRED, updated automatically every week.
  • Trade and products: Taiwan Ministry of Economic Affairs, January 2004 to December 2024. "China" means mainland China only. Hong Kong is not included.
  • Industry: Taiwan Ministry of Economic Affairs industrial production index by industry, 2004 to 2024.
  • Export orders and investment: Taiwan Ministry of Economic Affairs, 2003 to 2024. For export orders, "China" includes Hong Kong.
